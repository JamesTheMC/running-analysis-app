Capture guide: filming a running clip the app can trust

Follow this for every clip. Numbers marked * are provisional: chosen for this guide, to be confirmed at the next filming session. Unmarked numbers are app requirements.

Why it matters

  • The app measures joint angles from the video, on this device. The camera setup decides whether those numbers can be trusted.
  • 16 earlier clips (8 runners) were filmed from floor level with a wide lens, and the treadmill console hid the hips. The app rightly refused all of them. A good setup takes two minutes.

Phone settings

  • Frame rate: 60 fps minimum; 120 fps if the phone offers it. The app analyses at 60 Hz (every 2nd frame of a 120 fps clip). More than 120 fps is not needed.
  • Older 30 fps clips still pass the video check, with a note: contact timing is less precise (±33 ms).
  • Main 1x lens, not the ultra-wide 0.5x. No zoom: move the tripod instead.
  • Landscape (phone on its side). Use the phone's own camera file, not a screen recording.

Camera setup

  • Tripod, never hand-held. Camera level (no tilt up or down) and square to the belt.
  • Height: lens level with the runner's hip (the bony bump on the side, greater trochanter), within about ±10 cm*. On most treadmills that is about 1.1–1.2 m* above the floor.
  • Side views: lens pointing straight across the belt (at 90° to it), aimed at the runner's hip.
  • Rear / front: centred on the belt midline, lens pointing straight along the belt.
  • Distance: about 3.5 m* from lens to runner (range 3–4 m*), side and rear. Whole body in frame, head to feet, including the foot at the back of the swing, with a margin of about 10%* of the frame on every side. The runner fills at least half the frame height.

Running and visibility

  • Warm up first (about 5 min*). Start recording once the speed is steady.
  • Record 30–60 s (30 s minimum; 60 s* is plenty) at one constant speed per clip. Note the speed and incline.
  • Console and handrails must not hide the hips, knees or feet. If they do, move the camera, don't film anyway.
  • Shorts or tights, fitted top; shirt tucked or short so the pelvis shows from behind. For the rear view, two small tape dots on the dimples at the base of the back help (optional).
  • Good, even light; no bright window or light behind the runner. Plain, uncluttered background; nobody else in the frame.

Views (top-down; runner runs towards the console)

Four views, any subset. Ideal: three, e.g. left + right + rear, or one side + rear + front. One camera position per clip.

File name

CODE_YYYY-MM-DD_VIEW_SPEED, e.g. T3_2026-10-20_left_6.0mph.mov. VIEW is left, right, rear or front.

Client code only, never a name. Videos stay on the device: the app does not upload them.

Before you press record

  • Tripod, landscape, 1x lens, no zoom
  • 60 fps or more (120 if offered)
  • Lens at hip height, camera level and square
  • Whole body in frame with a margin all round, even at the back of the swing
  • Hips, knees and feet not hidden by console or rails
  • Even light, plain background, nobody else in view
  • Runner warmed up, speed steady; record 30–60 s

This is movement analysis to support clinical judgment, not a diagnosis. Any clip the app judges unsuitable gets no values, only the reason and re-filming advice.
